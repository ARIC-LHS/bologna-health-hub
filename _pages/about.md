  <h1>Bologna: an ideal ecosystem for health research</h1>

  <p class="lede">
    The Bologna metropolitan area offers an ideal environment for health
    research. Its ecosystem brings together the University of Bologna
    (UNIBO), a comprehensive university with a long tradition of
    multidisciplinary research, and three research hospitals recognised
    by the Italian Ministry of Health as Scientific Institutes for
    Research, Hospitalisation and Healthcare (IRCCS).
  </p>

  <p>
    The three research hospitals are the IRCCS Azienda
    Ospedaliero-Universitaria di Bologna (IRCCS AOUBO), the IRCCS
    Istituto Ortopedico Rizzoli (IRCCS IOR), and the IRCCS Istituto
    delle Scienze Neurologiche di Bologna (IRCCS ISN). IRCCS status
    identifies institutions of scientific and clinical excellence
    dedicated to biomedical and health-system research alongside
    highly specialised healthcare.
  </p>

  <p>
    Together, the four institutions combine fundamental, translational
    and clinical research, advanced research infrastructures, extensive
    patient populations and cohorts, technology development, and
    participation in European research networks. This portal presents
    the ecosystem from a matchmaking perspective for Horizon Europe
    Cluster 1 Health.
  </p>

    <p class="eyebrow">UNIBO</p>

    <h2>Alma Mater Studiorum – University of Bologna</h2>

    <p>
      The University of Bologna, the oldest university in the Western
      world, combines a long academic tradition with cutting-edge
      research, an extensive curriculum and a broad international
      perspective.
    </p>

    <p>
      According to the <strong>CENSIS Ranking of Italian Universities
      2026–2027, UNIBO ranks second among Italian
      mega-universities</strong>. It also holds leading national
      positions in several disciplinary areas.
    </p>

    <p>
      UNIBO promotes gender equality, inclusion and diversity through
      its
      <a href="https://www.unibo.it/en/university/statute-standards-strategies-and-reports/gender-equality-plan">
        Gender Equality Plan 2025–2027
      </a>.
      The Plan addresses work-life balance, organisational culture,
      equality in leadership and recruitment, gender mainstreaming in
      research and teaching, and the prevention of gender-based
      violence and harassment.
    </p>

    <p>
      UNIBO is a Euraxess Contact Point. The University's Quality
      Assurance System has been assessed by ANVUR.
    </p>

    <h3>Organisation and research community</h3>

    <p>
      UNIBO operates across five campuses and a branch in Buenos Aires.
      It offers 260 degree programmes through 31 Departments to a
      community of more than 97,000 students. A further 7,808 students
      are enrolled in postgraduate programmes, including 51 PhD
      programmes, 59 Specialisation Schools and 92 Professional
      Master's programmes.
    </p>

    <p>
      The scientific community includes <strong>3,423 professors and
      researchers</strong>: 1,008 full professors, 1,615 associate
      professors and 800 researchers. Research is organised across the
      University's 31 Departments and supported by multidisciplinary
      research structures, shared facilities and central services.
    </p>

    <h3>International standing</h3>

    <p>
      In the QS Europe University Rankings 2026, UNIBO ranks
      <strong>59th in Europe and second in Italy</strong>. Its academic
      reputation ranks 17th in Europe.
    </p>

    <p>
      In the QS World University Rankings by Subject 2026, UNIBO ranks
      first in Italy in 16 disciplines and among the world's Top 50 in
      nine disciplines. In Life Sciences &amp; Medicine, UNIBO ranks
      108th worldwide, with particularly strong results in Veterinary
      Science, Agriculture &amp; Forestry, and Medicine.
    </p>

    <h3>Sustainability, multidisciplinarity and innovation</h3>

    <p>
      The University's 2022–2027 Strategic Plan incorporates the
      <a href="https://sdgs.un.org/goals">
        17 United Nations Sustainable Development Goals
      </a>,
      integrating sustainability, equity, inclusion and social
      responsibility into its strategic objectives.
    </p>

    <p>
      Through European research and academic cooperation projects,
      UNIBO has developed extensive collaborations with universities,
      research institutions, industry and public and private
      organisations. It participates in regional, national and
      international networks and maintains institutional agreements
      supporting long-term research collaboration.
    </p>

    <p>
      UNIBO also has an extensive portfolio of patents and plant
      varieties and a well-established ecosystem for the creation and
      development of spin-offs and start-ups. Innovation and
      knowledge-transfer activities are supported by dedicated
      University structures and initiatives such as Almacube.
    </p>

    <h3>Health research areas</h3>

    <p>
      The University of Bologna is a comprehensive research university
      active across a broad range of Health and Digital Health domains.
      Its principal competency areas include:
    </p>

    <ul>
      <li>Ambient Assisted Living</li>
      <li>Big Data and Omics Medicine</li>
      <li>Brain Ageing and Neurodegeneration</li>
      <li>Cancer</li>
      <li>Digital medicine and eHealth</li>
      <li>Epidemiology, Biobanking and Cohorts</li>
      <li>Health, non-communicable diseases and the environment</li>
      <li>Multimorbidity and ageing</li>
      <li>Pharmaceutical biotechnology</li>
      <li>Rare diseases and undiagnosed disorders</li>
      <li>Regenerative medicine and tissue engineering</li>
      <li>Skeletal health and diseases</li>
      <li>Social and ethical aspects of health</li>
      <li>Systems and personalised medicine</li>
    </ul>

    <p>
      Further information is available on the
      <a href="https://www.unibo.it/en/research/projects-and-initiatives/horizon-europe">
        University of Bologna Horizon Europe pages
      </a>.
    </p>

    <h3>Research and innovation competitive funding</h3>

    <p>
      Competitive funding activities are supported throughout the
      project lifecycle by the University's Research Division. Support
      is provided in close cooperation with knowledge-transfer
      structures responsible for innovation management, intellectual
      property protection, exploitation and the uptake of research
      results.
    </p>

    <p>
      In Horizon Europe, <strong>238 UNIBO proposals have been invited
      for funding, corresponding to an EU contribution of approximately
      €115 million</strong>.
    </p>

    <p>
      In Health, UNIBO secured <strong>21 funded projects during
      2021–2026, including four as coordinator, attracting approximately
      €11.1 million</strong>. UNIBO ranks first among Italian
      universities in Horizon Europe Health. Its funding represents
      approximately 8% of the total obtained by Italian universities in
      this area.
    </p>

  <section id="aoubo">

    <p class="eyebrow">IRCCS AOUBO</p>

    <h2>IRCCS Azienda Ospedaliero-Universitaria di Bologna</h2>

    <p>
      IRCCS AOUBO is Bologna's principal university hospital, with more
      than 400 years of history. It is the main clinical venue of the
      University of Bologna's School of Medicine and Surgery and is a
      reference centre for healthcare activities essential to academic
      education and clinical research.
    </p>

    <p>
      The hospital is a multispecialist, highly specialised institution
      distributed across 32 pavilions. It has <strong>1,371 beds and
      5,971 employees</strong>, including university researchers and
      physicians.
    </p>

    <p>
      Each year, IRCCS AOUBO admits approximately 58,000 patients and
      provides an estimated 4.3 million outpatient services. Healthcare
      and research activities are organised through 13 Departments of
      Integrated Activities and four inter-company departments.
    </p>

    <p>
      In 2020, the hospital was recognised by the Italian Ministry of
      Health as an IRCCS. Its two principal areas of scientific and
      clinical excellence are:
    </p>

    <ol>
      <li>
        Assistance and research in transplantation and critical
        patients.
      </li>
      <li>
        Integrated medical and surgical management of oncological
        diseases.
      </li>
    </ol>

    <p>
      Research spans preclinical, translational and clinical activities,
      including clinical trials from T1 to T4. IRCCS AOUBO hosts more
      than <strong>60 disease registries</strong>, numerous datasets for
      clinical and observational studies and participates in
      <strong>12 European Reference Networks</strong>.
    </p>

    <h3>Health research areas</h3>

    <p>
      Research is organised through eight lines:
    </p>

    <ol>
      <li>
        Translational and clinical approaches for the early diagnosis
        and prevention of complications in solid-organ and
        haematopoietic stem-cell transplantation.
      </li>
      <li>
        Prevention, diagnosis and treatment of severe organ failure and
        optimisation of transplantation.
      </li>
      <li>
        Reconditioning and regeneration of cells, tissues and organs
        and optimisation of donor resources.
      </li>
      <li>
        Multidisciplinary technologies and personalised therapeutic
        strategies for patients with organ failure.
      </li>
      <li>
        Diagnostic, prognostic and predictive markers in solid and
        haematological malignancies using omics and artificial
        intelligence.
      </li>
      <li>
        Advanced molecular and immunological therapies in cancer,
        including phase I and phase II clinical trials.
      </li>
      <li>
        Clinical development of innovative therapies for rare and
        highly complex solid and haematological cancers.
      </li>
      <li>
        CAR-T research, from the production of new constructs to the
        prediction of outcomes and complications.
      </li>
    </ol>

    <h3>Research and innovation competitive funding</h3>

    <p>
      In 2025, IRCCS AOUBO activated <strong>32 new competitively funded
      research projects</strong>, worth approximately €3.26 million.
      These included nine European and 23 national projects.
    </p>

    <p>
      In the same year, researchers at the Institute produced more than
      2,000 scientific publications. Of these, 937 were considered valid
      for the Italian Ministry of Health research evaluation.
    </p>

  <section id="ior">

    <p class="eyebrow">IRCCS IOR</p>

    <h2>IRCCS Istituto Ortopedico Rizzoli</h2>

    <p>
      Founded in 1896, the Istituto Ortopedico Rizzoli is an
      international reference centre for orthopaedics and
      musculoskeletal research. It has been recognised by the Italian
      Ministry of Health as a scientific research hospital since 1981
      and is the only public Italian IRCCS specifically recognised in
      Orthopaedics.
    </p>

    <p>
      Its model integrates three clinical departments with
      <strong>nine biomedical and technological research
      laboratories</strong>. This integration supports the translation
      of scientific and technological research into new diagnostic,
      surgical, rehabilitative and therapeutic approaches.
    </p>

    <p>
      IOR coordinates the IRCCS Musculoskeletal System Network and
      participates in the IRCCS Aging and Alliance Against Cancer
      networks. It also participates in the European Reference Network
      for adult solid cancers, EURACAN, and coordinates the European
      Reference Network for rare bone diseases, ERN BOND.
    </p>

    <p>
      The Institute is a regional reference centre for musculoskeletal
      tumours, vertebral surgery, paediatric orthopaedics, foot and
      upper-limb surgery, prosthetic revision and replacement, and the
      treatment of severe bone infections. It is also a reference
      centre for rare skeletal disorders.
    </p>

    <p>
      IOR hosts the Emilia-Romagna Musculoskeletal Tissue Bank, which
      provides a substantial share of the musculoskeletal tissue used
      in Italy for implants and transplants. It also manages RIPO, the
      Orthopaedic Prosthetic Implant Register, and REPO, the associated
      Explant Register.
    </p>

    <h3>Health research areas</h3>

    <p>
      IOR research is organised around four principal translational
      lines:
    </p>

    <ol>
      <li>
        <strong>Musculoskeletal Oncology</strong>: tumour biology,
        diagnostic and predictive biomarkers, personalised medicine,
        registries and biobanking.
      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Regenerative and Reconstructive Orthopaedics</strong>:
        biomaterials, coatings, scaffolds, grafts, stem cells,
        extracellular vesicles and advanced preclinical models.
      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Innovative technologies for musculoskeletal
        surgery</strong>: digital modelling, artificial intelligence,
        robotics, surgical navigation, bionic limbs, augmented reality
        and digital twins.
      </li>
      <li>
        <strong>Inflammatory, infectious, degenerative and genetic
        musculoskeletal diseases</strong>: prevention, diagnosis,
        pharmacological and rehabilitative treatments, ageing,
        infections and rare diseases.
      </li>
    </ol>

    <p>
      Additional areas of expertise include personalised surgical
      planning, medical devices, biomaterials, nanotechnologies,
      orthobiology, regenerative medicine, osteointegration, bionic
      prostheses, bioprinting, advanced imaging, in-silico medicine and
      predictive biomarkers.
    </p>

    <h3>Research and innovation competitive funding</h3>

    <p>
      As of January 2025, IOR had <strong>104 active funded research
      projects</strong>. Of these, 51 were funded through competitive
      calls, including nine European projects.
    </p>

    <p>
      Since 2022, IOR has served as Competent Authority in the
      EU4Health Joint Action JARDIN, which supports the integration of
      European Reference Networks into national healthcare systems.
    </p>

    <p class="eyebrow">IRCCS ISN</p>

    <h2>IRCCS Istituto delle Scienze Neurologiche di Bologna</h2>

    <p>
      The Istituto delle Scienze Neurologiche di Bologna is a centre of
      excellence for translational neuroscience, connecting fundamental
      research with adult and paediatric neurology, neurosurgery,
      neuroradiology, emergency neurology and neurorehabilitation.
    </p>

    <p>
      The Institute brings together <strong>more than 300 researchers
      across more than 40 clinical units</strong>. More than 1,000
      square metres are dedicated to neuroscience laboratories,
      alongside the Bologna Neuroscience Biobank and advanced
      infrastructures for molecular and cellular biology, sequencing,
      proteomics, mass spectrometry, neuroimaging and neurosurgery.
    </p>

    <p>
      The scientific production of the Institute over the last decade
      includes more than 2,100 contributions in neuroscience journals.
      Since 2013, the Institute has attracted more than €62 million in
      research funding from public and private sources.
    </p>

    <p>
      ISN participates in four European Reference Networks, the Italian
      IRCCS Paediatric Network and coordinates the Italian IRCCS
      Neuroscience and Rehabilitation Network. It is also a member of
      the ENIGMA Consortium.
    </p>

    <h3>Health research areas</h3>

    <p>
      Research is organised around four lines approved by the Italian
      Ministry of Health:
    </p>

    <ol>
      <li>
        Neurological and neuropsychiatric diseases of adulthood and
        brain ageing, including genetics, pathogenesis, deep
        phenotyping and personalised therapy.
      </li>
      <li>
        Neurological and neuropsychiatric diseases of developmental
        age, including genetics, pathogenesis, deep phenotyping and
        personalised therapy.
      </li>
      <li>Emergency neurology.</li>
      <li>
        Neurosurgery, nervous-system oncology and oncological
        neuropathology.
      </li>
    </ol>

    <p>
      The Institute has internationally recognised expertise in liquid,
      tissue and imaging biomarkers of neurodegenerative diseases,
      genetics and epigenetics, brain ageing, neuroimaging and the
      integration of biological markers with functional-connectivity
      measures.
    </p>

    <h3>Neurorehabilitation and bioengineering</h3>

    <p>
      The Rehabilitation Bioengineering Laboratory integrates
      bioengineering, neuroscience and clinical research. It develops
      and validates robotic systems, brain-computer interfaces,
      artificial-intelligence tools, virtual-reality applications,
      wearable sensors and human-machine interfaces for motor and
      cognitive rehabilitation.
    </p>

    <p>
      The Laboratory brings together researchers from the University of
      Bologna's Department of Electrical, Electronic and Information
      Engineering, clinical engineers and ISN researchers. Its work
      supports mobility, cognitive training, assistive technologies,
      independence and quality of life for people with disabilities.
    </p>

    <h3>Research and innovation competitive funding</h3>

    <p>
      ISN participates in Horizon Europe, EU4Health, ERN, European
      Partnership for Personalised Medicine, Joint Programme for
      Neurodegenerative Disease Research and Innovative Health
      Initiative projects, as well as research funded by the Italian
      Ministry of Health and private research foundations.
    </p>

    <p>
      In 2024, ISN was involved in 53 nationally and internationally
      funded projects. Since 2022, it has participated in 69 for-profit
      clinical trials and is accredited for phase I clinical trials.
    </p>
